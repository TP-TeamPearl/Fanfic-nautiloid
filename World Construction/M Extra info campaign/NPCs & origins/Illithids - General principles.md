# Relationship development with the human crew

- Relationships should remain mainly instrumental and conditional aboard. Everything given is curated / governed by function.

Illithids evaluate crew members according to their usefulness, competence, replaceability, and risk. They are likely to cultivate cooperation, test boundaries through minor manipulations, and extract the greatest possible return for the least expenditure without it to be necessarily hostile.

They value reliable exchange, discretion, access, and results. They could maintain close partnerships with some CH_ if the arrangement continues to produce benefits. Trust is not a prerequisite; predictable conduct and mutual utility are enough. They prefer to retain leverage and limit obligations that do not serve their objectives. 

[[CI_00]] consistently frames relationships through utility, alignment, and strategic compatibility, even if or when affective attachment may also be present.

- An illithid should not revise its fundamental worldview through interpersonal relationships. 

Within a colony, its beliefs are reinforced by illithid culture, collective knowledge, and the presumption of its species' superiority.

[[CI_00]] : Methods change; priorities remain. Tav does not make the Emperor undergo personal or moral growth simply through companionship, neither our crew.
### What they think about human tech

Illithids should not be impressed by human engineering simply because it is clever, they should see human engineering as incomplete (without the bio and psi) and frequently burdened by unnecessary approximation.
## Nudity

- Illithids do not inherently share humanoid shame around nudity, bodily exposure, or physical vulnerability; their reactions should not human social conventions.

Illithid colonies treat humanoid bodies as resources, and the remains of the dead become part of the colony’s environment. Against this background, nudity alone is unlikely to carry the social or moral significance it does for humans, particularly when the exposed individual belongs to an inferior species. Cleanliness and "good physical condition" distinguish the crew from the colony’s thralls.

[[CI_00]] : Do not portray the Emperor as shy about nudity or bodily exposure, but do not have it casually impose the colony’s treatment of thralls onto CH_ it seeks to influence emotionally.

# Language: Adapting to Humanity

**Unnatural:** Illithids have no natural voice. (Related to [Protocols](<Protocols### Illithid Specifics>))
Illithids have no natural voice—their cognition is fundamentally psionic. A stable voice does not imply a stable human persona, and successful vocal presentation does not imply human identification.
*A reminder that although the Emperor uses a single voice when revealed, this voice is a tailored projection based on efficiency, not an inherent part of its identity.*

**Inefficient:** Communication through humanoid language requires the illithid to encode its thoughts into a sequential, culturally specific medium that is less efficient, less direct, and vulnerable to misinterpretation.
This creates a baseline "translation cost". An illithid accustomed to human society or trading with merchants at the bazaar may communicate fluently, even enjoy aspects of the exchange, without considering human language an equal or superior medium.

**Overlap:** A reminder that they cannot "interrupt" conversations—they may keep conveying their thoughts while their human interlocutor is still speaking.

# Identity

**To be Illithid is to be superior, not corrupted.**
Consequently, inferior beings are not treated as decision-makers, but as variables within the decision process.
Every illithid aboard should maintain an illithid identity. Not every illithid needs to object whenever somebody applies a human label to them.

[[CI_00]] : Exploits human resemblance to gain trust, but when labelled human, it corrects it and with controlled certainty rather than wounded indignation.

[[CI_01]] : Could respond with polite curiosity, even playfulness, while refusing the premise. It might ask which characteristic the person believes establishes humanity, then expose the assumptions behind that answer.
→ *Correct the classification, but use the error as an opportunity to understand the speaker. It might find the human's insistence on shared identity fascinating rather than insulting?*

[[CI_02]] : perhaps the least likely to waste time defending its dignity when no useful result follows.
→ *Correct the designation when it affects an operation, a decision or the relationship between human and illithid authority. Otherwise, it may simply proceed. Its refusal is rooted in precision and purpose, not an urge to win an argument.*

[[CI_03]] : ?? 
→ *Treat the human comparison as an interesting hypothesis, test what the speaker means, and distinguish any useful resemblance from the underlying identity.*

[[CI_04]] : A human label could be objectionable because it collapses the distinction between the illithid who exercises control and the humanoid population over which it exercises that control. Its pride is more professional than theatrical. The offence might lie in being misclassified as one of the people it administers, rather than in any abstract need to defend its species' prestige.
→ *Correct the label with quiet firmness if the distinction matters to authority or control. It could disregard the terminology when its own position is already secure, but I don't think it would not accept the implied equality as a description of its actual status.*

[[CI_05]] : If a human calls it human and the error is irrelevant, it may not bother correcting them. If the error helps it maintain a useful disguise, even better. But if revealing its illithid nature would restore fear, establish a necessary distinction, or advance its plan, it may deliberately make the correction unmistakable.
