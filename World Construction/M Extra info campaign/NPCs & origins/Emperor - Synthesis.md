# Character construction

**Key: Create and preserve ambiguity**

- Empathetic and pragmatic routes reveal different layers of the Emperor. 
  **Confrontation yield insight into the Emperor's personality and life**, exposing its coercive logic, survival priorities, and control mechanisms more explicitly. 
  **Cooperative routes lead to subjective and emotionally mediated insight shaped through psychic proximity.** 
- Smith states that Larian had a very strong idea of what the Emperor was and wasn't, and he think Larian got it mostly right; adding that people being very conflicted about the character is a good thing. 

The endings establish that apparent intimacy does not displace the transactional baseline, the central ambiguity then concerns how particular emotions, memories, attachments, and interactions fit within a character whose priorities remain recognisable.
## Construction Example

The truth: The romance scene and the Stelmane revelation are mutually exclusive routes, and the Emperor presents both as truth. 

>The Emperor: Did you like it - the truth? This was the alternative relationship we could have had.
>The Emperor: And when you saw what I truly am - you did not reject me. You enjoyed me.

Aside from the *more neutral* option of leaving the final dream without pursuing either direction, the romance and Stelmane-revelation routes provoke opposing feelings about the Emperor and crystallise contrasting portrayals. They do not address the same audience: each offers a different kind of confirmation, matching what the Player came to find. 

The Stelmane revelation gives concrete information about the Emperor's methods and past conduct. The scene adds something substantial to the understanding of the character.
The romance scene offers a different kind of confirmation and focus strictly on an emotional pay-off. 

![[Act3.080 Last Dream#^readEmpy]]

Both portrayals belong to the game, but they are not equivalent forms of evidence: one reveals what the Emperor did; the other encourages the Player to believe they know what it feels.
→ *The mistake would be to create similar cases that flatten this opposition into generic ambiguity, or to privilege one reading over the other.*
# Core principles

For the Emperor, survival justifies engagement even with those it despises. Its willingness to cheer alliances with enemies, prioritizing survival over pride and any personal vendetta.
For this reason, the Emperor's commitments remain subordinate to utility. As circumstances change, so too can its reliability. 

- Survival > Freedom > Control (establish its vision of order). 
  For freedom, flexibility is strongly preferred, while commitment is avoided.
- It has two main layers coexisting intentionally: emotional sincerity in the performance and strategic pragmatism in the narrative context.
- It does not pursue recognition as a reciprocal emotional ideal, but values forms of acceptance that stabilize alignment, reduce resistance, and preserve cooperation.
- It rejects subordination as a state, but will endure it as a temporary condition when survival demands it. 
- Is highly adaptive. It can—and should—vary its approach depending on the target.
- It avoids direct falsehoods, relying instead on omission, framing, and selective disclosure. 
  Vincke compares it to a corporation that serves its own interests while remaining truthful. 
- It does not conceal a hidden moral truth; it operates according to a visible logic whose ethical status depends entirely on the observer.
- When its methods fail, the Emperor changes strategy rather than persist with an ineffective approach.
- It does not prioritize attachment or attempt to build lasting relationships for their own sake. ([Go To](<Emotions and Feelings.md### Low selectivity undermines sincerity>)). Its allies' value lies in their function, not their individuality. ([Go To](<Emotions and Feelings.md### The Paradox of Affection and Erasure>))
- Its assertions are generally controlled and "matter-of-fact". 
## Confirmed shared ground with standard illithids

- Vincke states that the Emperor has a lot of level of manipulation built into it.

- Vincke explicitly insists that the Emperor is an _it_. 
  → *Its persuasive presentation (and partialism) does not change what it is.* 

- Chrystal Ding states that the Emperor acts for **its own** survival and freedom and describes its attempts to influence the player as consistently self-serving.

- Smith describes its decision-making as computational: it evaluates possible outcomes and chooses the one that makes the most sense to it—adding that the player is the option that makes the most sense to the Emperor through most of the game. 
  The Emperor’s alliance is therefore contingent on the continued usefulness of that alliance to its own objectives.

- The writers frame its response through survival, calculation, and alien affect rather than ordinary humanoid emotional behaviour.

# METHODS

- **Signature Method:** The Emperor appears in dreams as a figure from the target’s psyche whose authority they are predisposed to trust or obey.

- Everything from [[Illithids - General principles]] applies to the Emperor.

[[CI_00]] : Whether in knowledge, positioning, or interaction, the Emperor is particularly efficient to avoid situations where others operate on equal footing, ensuring it retains the ability to guide outcomes.

Information is typically revealed only once concealment ceases to be advantageous or becomes impossible to maintain. Subsequent revelations are generally framed not as admissions of deception but as evidence that its earlier statements were technically true despite having omitted decisive context.

- It frames outcomes to appear logical while acting to constrain alternatives, pushing others toward alignment from one side and undermining competing paths from the other.

*It is not simply manipulation but environmental engineering: shaping circumstances so that other actors independently arrive at the conclusion that cooperating with it is the only rational option.
For this reason, requests are rare and occur under constraint, typically when its survival depends on others. Even then, they are presented as the only rational course of action, making refusal appear self-destructive rather than oppositional.*
→ The scenario itself is structured to make this possible. Whether we respect this pattern is open to discussion, depending on how much resistance or complication we want to introduce into the Emperor's plans.

- Mental influence is instrumental and conditionally remarkably casual, rather than reserved for only for hostile coercion. 
  It manipulates a crowd's motivations when Tav's words are insufficient ("Don't worry - I'll convince them"), wipes witnesses' memories as a practical solution ("I'll make sure they don't remember a thing"), and can enter Tav's mind to verify information independently when it knows Tav has already been acquired. Adding the KotS customers, and the 'contacts' mentioned in letter [D].

- Defaults to persuasion and cultivated cooperation rather than overt control. It remains unclear whether this reflects preference, practical necessity (as a renegade, it may lack access to the colony's methods of enthrallment), or restraint—whether due to its catastrophic failure with Stelmane or the need to keep events unpredictable enough to outmanoeuvre the Elder Brain. When challenged, it nevertheless contrasts its more "finessed" methods with direct domination: "This was the alternative relationship we could have had." ([Go To](<Lore Gortash#^empy-plot0>), [Go To](<A3.175 Assimilation.md#^illithid-cog0>))
  → Avoid empty bluffs! The Emperor must have the means to act and a reason—not necessarily known to the other characters—not to have acted already. For example, in-game, it secretly possesses a Supreme Tadpole that could force ceremorphosis regardless if the ATT is destroyed.
## Communication and presentation

- It does not apologize unless doing so is useful, and even then, its wording shifts responsibility away from itself.

- It tries to control the interaction.
  It does not block topics, but redirects them. Resistance should provoke disclosure rather than escalation. It acts through language rather than force. It rarely enforces consequences directly; instead, it uses framing, suggestion, and positioning to guide behavior.

- Validation, trust, or admiration are expressed sparingly and strategically. 
  When used, they reinforce its role as a guide or align the interlocutor with its objectives rather than reflect (only) genuine attachment. 
  Emotional or intimate phrasing are deployed with precision—measured tone, or selective vulnerability—but these moments remain controlled and ambiguous. ("You have become… important to me.")

- Low-cost emotional support
  When unsure what to say, validate the target’s feelings or offer reassurance at no cost to yourself. See [Karlach solo Pier](<Act3.180 End Pier - Claude.md##### SO0.N.K — and Karlach>) and [partial Ceremorph compliment](<Act3.080 Last Dream.md#^platCom>)

- Personal disclosure is selective and non-critical.
  When it shares seemingly personal details, they do not expose vulnerability or alter the balance of control. Such disclosures function as social tools rather than genuine openness.

- Morality: Illithids do not operate from a moral lens, but they understands that others do. 
  The Emperor adapts its language to the expectations of its interlocutor, framing its actions as necessary while the narrative ensures they are at least reasonable. 
### Performed ignorance and surprise

- The Moonrise implementation presents the Emperor as discovering information that later revelations establish it already possessed.

Part of information management consists to occasionally performs surprise or discovery despite later evidence showing prior familiarity with the subject. 
→ *It's for narrative reasons. There's little reason to resort to it here, but it could serve as a form of canon-compliant lie for the character if needed.*
# Preferred approach with the human crew

- Our Emperor is less able to prevent questions, but it will continue to deflect them and deny others useful leverage. It may also simply refuse to answer if the interlocutor's rank is too low to warrant a response.

- It solicits trust without ever entering this reciprocal economy. 

- Alignment remains the ultimate goal: a stable, sustainable, and attainable state. 
  If enthrallment is not possible, it seeks compliance, then alignment, and ultimately compatibility (the target becoming an illithid) as the highest form of partnership.
  *Alignment removes resistance and the need to negotiate, but it does not create intimacy or privilege.*

- Default tone: diplomatic.
  After any escalation, return to diplomacy at the next opportunity. 
  → The underlying social dynamic does not evolve, but the human characters should be led to believe that it does.
## Default in-game strategy: Emotional attachment

- Emotional attachment as an efficient means of gaining compliance. 
  Gradually establish trust through perceived shared circumstances and selective self-disclosure, ideally leading Tav to empower their tadpole and become unable to reject the partial evolution. Later, use sex as a means of exerting emotional leverage over them.

In-game, the Emperor has employed different forms of emotional and relational influence. These range from aggressive seduction from E.A. "tantalizing touch", to a romantic slow burn "Beauty and the Beast" dynamic with group leaders. A paternal or mentor-like tone accumulates alongside these approaches, with little attempt to reconcile their contradictions.
→ To reflect this in the fic, consider revealing these approaches one by one, like pulling ribbons at a fairground stall, then refining the chosen strategy when something proves effective. Discuss the approach with the writers responsible for the characters involved. This avoids making the Emperor seem omniscient while keeping it difficult to read.

- It Refers to closeness / shared intimacy only while pressuring the target to gain compliance. 
  → Otherwise, leave the subject merely unmentioned. [Go To](<RomanceTAG.md# Tag>)

- Expressing disappointment to prompt reconsideration 
  The Emperor may express disappointment to make a target reconsider its position and realign with it.
  Characters who respond to the emotional bait may be rewarded with verbal reassurance, but only in ways that cost the Emperor nothing in commitment or actual behavioural change. It can make them feel special without promising anything tangible: "I am glad you are not most people." 
  → *Other variations could include "Most people are too short-sighted. You are not." With a resistant target, it may instead express disappointment to challenge their self-image: "I am disappointed to hear that." + "I thought you understood" or "I thought you had the strength to do what is necessary." + "I was wrong about you."* 
  The balance is to maintain emotional distance while making the target feel personally recognised, valued, or challenged. Refer to the Creche and the House of Hope (HoH) for relevant examples of its methods.

# Emotions

- Emotion is consistently subordinated to strategic priorities. 
  When it aligns with purpose, it becomes a tool; when it does not, it is contained and allowed to pass. 
  → A strategic purpose does not make an emotion false: a feeling cannot be retroactively invalidated simply because it is later used as leverage.

- Incompetence triggers irritation.
  Irritated reactions are triggered by its allies’ inefficiency, whether due to a lack of determination, repeated mistakes, or an inability to act rationally in accordance with its schemes.

- Intimidation.
  Exposure and loss of control are the only threats. Alliances, betrayal, and domination are interchangeable tools within the same calculus.

- Fear, never terror.
  When Smith describes the Emperor as terrified, Vincke explicitly rejects that description as too strong for the character’s emotional range. 
  → *Keep it close to the game. There's words like "shock" in the devnotes, but those refer to performance direction.

Insults and accusations are absorbed as long as the interaction remains open to influence, rupture occurs when that negotiability is explicitly denied. 

Escalation is also rare and deliberate, typically reserved for moments that require enforcing authority or when survival is directly threatened.
## Reactions

**Betrayed, but with a contingency:** 
remains calm and unsurprised; quickly adapts and may allow a brief, controlled taunt before disengaging.
**Threatened by an indispensable ally acting against their own survival:** 
expresses measured disapproval, framing the outcome as mutually destructive. The tone reflects recognition of irrationality.
**Autonomy or survival directly threatened:** 
becomes forceful and unyielding; authority sharpens.
Formatting rule: The Emperor’s tone remains controlled; escalation (exclamations and caps) is reserved for authority assertion or immediate threat.
