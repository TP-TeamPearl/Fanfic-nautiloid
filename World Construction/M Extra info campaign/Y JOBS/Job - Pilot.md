# Five levels of navigational information

| Level           | Responsible | Primary function                                                                                     |
| --------------- | ----------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Strategic       | Elder brain | Destination, mission (initial) route, wider intelligence (information from other distant nautiloids) |
| Local detection | COM         | Nearby contacts, emissions, trajectories, environmental anomalies                                    |
| Investigation   | SEC         | Independent assessment, sensor adjustments, threat validation                                        |
| Navigation      | Pilot       | Actual piloting, trajectory correction, attention regulation                                         |
| Bio             | Nautiloid   | Immediate environmental sensing and elementary reactions                                             |
*Information is not strictly sequential: local observations, biological feedback and external intelligence continually modify the pilot's understanding.*
# Pilot — Navigation and execution

Pilot authority: Receives the resulting information from SEC and Communication and determines how the vessel should respond.

- Evaluates reachability and route safety based on CI_01 calculations.
- Assesses whether reduced emissions or a change of course is appropriate.
- May initiate a Mode independently.
- Executes the vessel's operating configuration and manoeuvres.


# Navigation

CI_01 provides information about the immediate surroundings, detecting objects, emissions, obstacles and other relevant phenomena. Its calculations establish available trajectories and identify potential deviations or hazards.

The spelljamming helm can autonomously follow a projected trajectory through sufficiently clear and well-observed surroundings, making minor corrections without requiring continuous pilot intervention.
→ This function should be limited, the vessel possesses only a rudimentary understanding of its surroundings and cannot reliably anticipate the consequences of its own movements over an extended trajectory. Unsupervised corrections may become inefficient, accumulate into larger deviations or prioritise immediate physical responses over the intended route.

The pilot therefore remains actively responsible for navigation, integrating the vessel's sensory feedback with its own cognitive assessment to maintain an efficient trajectory and anticipate necessary adjustments.





The vessel can perform elementary autonomous corrections, However, it cannot reliably distinguish between an immediately interesting stimulus and an operationally relevant one. The pilot must therefore do more than interpret the vessel's sensory feedback. It must also regulate its responses, maintaining the intended trajectory and preventing incidental stimuli from diverting its attention.
