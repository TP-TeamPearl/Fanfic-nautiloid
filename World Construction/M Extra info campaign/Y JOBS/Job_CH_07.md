## Role
His primary work aboard the nautiloid concerns the interface between humanoid neurological function, psionics and engineered machinery.

He investigates how neuronal signals can be translated, amplified or reproduced through artificial systems, with particular interest in:
- neural signals as an input to machinery;
- compatibility between humanoid cognition and illithid devices;
- experimental prosthetics, sensors and control mechanisms;

He works closely with [[CH_06]], who assists with procedures, preparation and observation.

### Primary Work — Human Helm Interface
[[CH_07]] is assigned to develop and stabilise the human interface at [[L_75 The Helm]].
The existing modifications allow [[CH_00]] to occupy an illithid pilot station, but the translated controls remain largely incomplete. Most systems respond inconsistently and produce partial outputs [[CH_00]] cannot interpret quickly enough for practical navigation.

His immediate objective is therefore not to make the helm _comfortable_ for a human, but to determine **which parts of illithid neural interaction can be translated into a form a humanoid brain can sustain**.

His work focuses increasingly on the brain itself:
- how the human brain responds to sustained contact with bridged illithid controls;
- which cognitive functions can substitute for missing illithid processing;
- how much information a humanoid operator can receive before performance collapses;
- whether neural activity can be redirected, suppressed, or temporarily repurposed as processing capacity;
- how to reduce the translation layer between human cognition and native nautiloid systems.

## Medical Officer
[[CH_07]] serves as the nautiloid's human medical officer because the human complement requires a designated physician. His background is broad enough to handle routine medicine, trauma, diagnostics, emergency treatment, and general surgical care, although he is not a specialist in every field.
His actual expertise lies in **neurobiology, experimental medicine and human–illithid interface research**. Medical duty therefore occupies only part of his role.
