# The Nautiloid as Wilderness 
He treats the nautiloid less like a ship and more like an unexplored ruin / hostile ecosystem. He respects living systems, but does not trust the nautiloid. Its biology feels wrong to him: rooms disappear, flesh consumes old structures, passages change, and its internal rhythms resemble a creature rather than a machine. He gradually treats the vessel like a sick  animal.

- Drawn to sealed rooms, dead corridors, forgotten machinery and inaccessible spaces. 
- Reads changes in walls, smells, temperature, sounds, vibrations and the ship's biological rhythms. 
- Builds a mental map through stories and landmarks rather than precise diagrams. 
- Remembers routes through narrative/tribal mnemonics: places have a smell, sound, rhythm, or story. 
- Can navigate even when conventional maps become unreliable or if the vessel changes.
