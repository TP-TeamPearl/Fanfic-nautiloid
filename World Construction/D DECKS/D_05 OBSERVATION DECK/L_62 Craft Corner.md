# L_62 Craft Corner
A small work corner is partitioned from the rest of the room by an industrial metal privacy screen. The structure resembles a rigid grid rather than a solid wall, offering only partial concealment while allowing light and movement to pass through.

Behind the screen stands a compact artificer bench dedicated to precision work. The surface is equipped with jeweler’s tools—fine clamps, polishing wheels, engraving needles, and adjustable lenses used for delicate assembly. Small furnaces and grinding stones allow the shaping and setting of gemstones or enchanted components into belts, buckles, shoulder fittings, and other equipment pieces.
The station is used primarily for finishing enchanted objects rather than creating them from raw materials. Here magical tools are refined, runic pens adjusted, and scroll cylinders sealed after inscription.
Despite its modest size, the bench contains everything necessary for careful work requiring steady hands and extreme precision.

# PH_01 Sync-Craft Interface
- Tech: Human
- Status: Prototype
##  Short: 
Also called "Mind Synchronizer", it enables a human artisan to work in tandem with an Illithid collaborator, utilizing a master-slave configuration. The master processes movements while the mind flayer mirrors these actions in real-time. This method gives practical experience instead of just memory from brain-eating. Missing finger is replaced by an altered version of the *Mage Hand* spell.
##  Long: 
A hybrid interface designed to transfer practical expertise without recourse to cerebral consumption. The device consists of a paired seating frame, positioning two subjects back to back: a humanoid artisan and an illithid receiver. Both are fitted with a cranial apparatus that enforces asymmetric psionic linkage.
The humanoid subject remains fully conscious and unrestrained, seated at a workbench equipped with tools and materials appropriate to their trade—most commonly fine jewelry, calibration components, or artefact housings. They are instructed to perform their craft as normal. No commands are issued beyond task selection.
The illithid subject is placed into a receptive, non-directive state. Motor output is not suppressed; instead, the device compels involuntary mirroring. As the artisan works, the illithid's hands repeat the same gestures in real time, guided not by observation but by transmitted motor intent. Surface thoughts, moment-to-moment decision points, and tactile corrections are received concurrently, stripped of narrative context and emotional framing.

This method allows the illithid to acquire executable skill rather than retrospective knowledge. Unlike cerebral ingestion, which yields static impressions and fragmented memory, synchronization forces the receiver to perform the action as it is understood, corrected, and refined by the artisan. Errors propagate immediately; adjustments are learned in motion.
Due to anatomical limitations, a sustained [[SPELLS# SH_00 Mage Hand|mage hand]] construct is employed to compensate for the illithid's missing finger. The construct is slaved to the illithid's motor impulses and functions purely as an extension, not an independent tool. Arcane theory is neither transmitted nor required.

The interface enforces a strictly one-directional transfer, with the illithid designated as the receiver. Sensory data, motor intent, and procedural judgment flow exclusively from the artisan to the illithid, while reverse intrusion is structurally inhibited. The device's geometry and positioning suppress distraction, allowing the artisan to work with the same focus and continuity as if alone. Eye contact and vocalization are made unnecessary.

*The Synchronizer is not intended for prolonged use. Extended sessions produce cognitive strain in both participants, including phantom motor impulses and delayed motor echo. Once sufficient procedural competence is acquired, the human subject is no longer required.*

- Recalibrated machinery [[CH_08]]:
	While assigned to [L_62] Craft Corner], the artisan recalibrates the interface using selected arcane artefacts, processing their extraction potential to allow the user to temporarily acquire limited spells.
	To achieve permanent acquisition, the user must successfully manifest the effect once per day for 30 consecutive days. Failure to meet this requirement results in the loss of the spell once the remaining uses are depleted. 
	
	Permanently acquired spells or cantrips are restructured into ritual forms. They require extended casting times and no longer require preparation or material components, rendering them unsuitable for immediate confrontation but valuable for controlled, utilitarian applications.
