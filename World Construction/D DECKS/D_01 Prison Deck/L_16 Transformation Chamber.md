A biomechanical neural apparatus surrounded by three stone tables. Each table connects a living subject directly to the central core through thick organic cables.

The subjects wear the robes of Ketheric's cultists from Moonrise. They are not restrained. Their minds have most likely been subdued before they were placed upon the tables. They are untadpoled(?)

The subjects' bodily functions seem to be reduced to the minimum necessary for survival, while their brains remain fully active. Are they feed or merely used and disposed?

- The system should fail without making the nautiloid helpless. Without the Brain Drain, the ship simply loses an extra sense.

The three subjects may be neurologically synchronized, they appear asleep or unresponsive, but their brains remain active (the masks regulate sedation and neural stimulation), with the central core receiving and correlating their neural activity (their dreams may occasionally overlap).

- Alternative: They were never tadpoled; the device requires intact, unaltered minds.

The vessel may need something primitive and constant: a tether to linear, embodied experience. Time passing normally? A body registering cold, fear, fatigue — mundane, ordinary sensation, the opposite of the Astral Sea's placelessness.
- then here it has to be a mind that's conscious, frightened, uncomfortable (not sedated, not looping in a drugged half-state). Fear and cold and exhaustion are proof of a body still anchored to normal time and space. The device wouldn't try harvesting intelligence but ordinary suffering, "present-tense" human mind, and using that as a kind of dead-reckoning compass to keep the ship oriented while it does something that shouldn't be navigable at all (in the Astral Sea).
