### Relationship with [[CH_07]]
She has worked beside her father for most of her life. Much of their communication is habitual rather than verbal: gestures, looks, prepared instruments, anticipated movements.
She is deeply attached to him and has learned to measure herself through usefulness to his work. His approval matters greatly, even when she knows his methods are questionable.

She is not blindly obedient. She notices when something is dangerous, excessive or unnecessary and may quietly object. Her instinct, however, is to remain useful rather than confront him.
Her dependence is therefore learned and emotional rather than imposed.

## Guqin
The best journey of [[CH_06]]'s childhood was one her family almost never had.
For once, both parents were able to put their work aside and travel together. They visited unfamiliar places, wandered without purpose, and gave her a freedom she would never experience in quite the same way again. For [[CH_06]], it became the memory of what a true family could feel like.

During the journey, the family passed through a large merchant bazaar. [[CH_06]] became fascinated by a guqin and insisted on having it. Her mother was reluctant to spend the money, but eventually gave in and bought it for her.

That evening, while the family was returning home, her mother died in a sudden accident. 
*CHECK! it could be random? A beast, a magical mishap, something escaped from a travelling spellcaster…*

The guqin was therefore the last thing her mother ever gave her. Learning to play became inseparable from the memory of that journey. Over time, the instrument became more than a keepsake: it was the one part of that day she could still actively recreate. Every time she plays, she preserves something that otherwise exists only in memory.
# Personality
Moral compartmentalization and aesthetic discipline.

[[CH_06]] is quiet, gentle and unusually attentive to other people. She is easy to trust, rarely confrontational, and tends to help without making herself the centre of attention.

Her softness is genuine, but it is not innocence.
She grew up assisting [[CH_07]] and became accustomed to experimental work from childhood. Corpses, surgical tools and biological specimens are therefore familiar objects rather than inherently disturbing ones. She learned to separate the person from the body on the table without necessarily losing compassion for the person.
This makes her surprisingly comfortable around death while remaining uncomfortable with cruelty.
### Surgical Mindset
[[CH_06]] approaches surgery through rhythm, sequence and precision. Her musical training reinforces the same habits: controlled movement, anticipation, economy and attention to subtle variation.
She does not need to become emotionally detached to perform invasive work. She simply treats the procedure as a discipline requiring concentration.
What unsettles her is not blood or death itself, but **unpredictability**. An incision can be precise. A suture can be perfect. A body can still react in a way she did not anticipate.
### Music
The guqin is one of the few things she keeps entirely for herself. She associates it with her mother, the exceptional childhood journey during which she received it, and the sudden death that ended that day. Music gives her a space where complexity remains structured. She can introduce variation deliberately rather than being forced to react to it.
# Misc
- [[Microcosm]]
Her conflict is between **compassion and usefulness**.
She has spent most of her life learning that being useful is how she stays close to the people she loves. She may become capable of almost anything if she can convince herself that it is necessary, precise, and useful.

[[CH_06]] is classified by the Banites as an **accompanying asset of [[CH_07]] rather than a full crew member**. Her pre-departure assessment focused on mental stability, reliability, and obedience to protocol, with little consideration given to her actual competence. Despite often matching or surpassing her father in surgical precision, she was granted **no formal authority or operational responsibility**.
