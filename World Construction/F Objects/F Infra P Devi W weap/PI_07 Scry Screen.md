
# PI_07 Scry Screen

An illithid observation device built around thin plates of translucent planar crystals held within a biomechanical frame. When inactive, the crystal is dark and unevenly reflective; when connected to a compatible sensory apparatus, shapes and movement emerge across its surface as a flattened visual representation of what the apparatus perceives.
The operator must settle a tentacle into the corresponding groove of the recessed control board before being able to use it, preventing creatures without illithid anatomy from accessing its functions. 

A geared mechanism mounted behind the screen controls a curved adjustment arc descending along its rear edge. Moving the arc physically alters the crystal's alignment and allows the operator to shift between different modes of perception, including ordinary visual representation and a red-tinted mode designed to emphasise living or otherwise detectable presences.

Numerous controls surround the frame. Rather than merely adjusting the image, they determine how incoming information is retained and compared. An operator may, for example, prevent previous detections from immediately fading from the crystal. Successive observations are then superimposed over these residual traces, allowing changes in position and movement to be compared over time.

To an untrained humanoid, extensive use of this function quickly turns the display into a confused accumulation of overlapping silhouettes and marks. Illithids are better able to interpret the layered information, treating the image less as a picture than as a record of successive states. 

The apparatus does not generate three-dimensional projections. Its output remains confined to the crystal surface.
