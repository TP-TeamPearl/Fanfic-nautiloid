GITH READ ME
Project Nautiloid — Baldur's Gate 3

A horror fan fiction set aboard the Emperor's nautiloid, developing events from its departure through the crash that marks the beginning of the canonical Baldur's Gate 3 campaign.

- Designed to be opened and worked on in Obsidian. 
- Just contribute; all inputs are welcome.
- Fork it when you want.

What can I do? Where do I start? What do I need to know?
You do not need to understand the whole project. 
You can add files to write scenes, insert your own characters, develop the environment, or simply contribute an idea.

If you're just dropping in, create a page, write your idea, and push it to the repository through your branch. You do not need to wait for the main writers before contributing or building on existing material.
Writing Quickest entry point is by creating your own character → [[START HERE]]

Take what you need. Reuse whatever fits your own story: the project is built to be reusable. You can fork it at any time to develop your own fic or campaign using the material already here.

(Details in [[Before contributing]] and [[Instruction]])

---

- This project is optimized to work with Obsidian software, though you should be able to add notes and push them from the repository.

- You should push the changes to Git yourself, but you can still send your text files (.md or .txt) to Rope by email.
  → *Since St is too lazy to handle the repository himself.*

- No moderation: we recommend to create a specific TeamPearl project ID (protonmail, facebook etc.) and keep the profiles privately.

This is a shared project, not a collection of individually owned stories: Anything contributed to the repository may be edited, expanded, rearranged, contradicted, or continued by other contributors.

  Asynchro: Someone might contribute intensely for three weeks and vanish for four months. So, characters, locations and plot elements may therefore develop while their creator is absent.
  Returning contributors can accept or adapt their material to what happened, or continue their own version as a fork. (Even after a fork, you are still welcome to return.)

Contributing therefore means accepting:
- the freedom of others to build on your work
- the freedom of the project to continue without you
- the freedom of contributors to use the project and its existing material as the basis for their own independent work/fork
